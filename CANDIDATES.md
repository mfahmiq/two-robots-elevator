# Candidate tournament — Two Robots in an Elevator

Method (Fahmi's patented rule, 25 Nov 2026): resubmits are allowed and each
round re-judges everything, and the judge is an AI pairwise judge whose brief
is published in full in RULES.txt — so the entry is chosen by an internal
pairwise tournament run against that brief's criteria, in their published
order:

1. Reads as one funny line of awkward small talk that one robot says to
   another in an elevator.
2. Sounds like a machine talking, with robot logic, sensors, firmware or
   charging habits showing through the politeness.
3. Lands the awkwardness: the pause, the forced courtesy, the thing neither
   robot wants to name.
4. Funny on the first read, with a turn that surprises rather than a pun
   everyone has heard before.
5. Tight as a single line, every word earning its place, no setup wasted.

Each match is judged the way the real judge judges: go through the criteria
in order, find the first one where the two lines differ, and let that decide
unless a later one differs by much more. Margin 1 = by a hair, 5 = overwhelming.

## Round 1 — single elimination (20 → 10)

- **#2 beats #1, margin 1.** Both are machine-logic anxiety, but #2 names the
  awkwardness directly ("still have no idea what to say in here") while #1's
  turn — pretending to recalculate a route — is cleverer yet lands softer.
  Criterion 3 decides it.
- **#4 beats #3, margin 3.** #3 is a weather pun everyone has heard; #4 turns
  charging — a robot habit — into a confession of wasted effort. Criteria 2
  and 4 both favour #4 by a lot.
- **#6 beats #5, margin 2.** #5 quantifies the silence nicely, but #6's
  rehearsed greetings, all unused, is funnier on the first read and the
  awkwardness is the whole line, not a measurement of it.
- **#8 beats #7, margin 2.** #7's music "judging us" is cute but generic and
  barely machine; #8's speech module timing out is robot logic failing at
  exactly the moment politeness is required.
- **#10 beats #9, margin 2.** #9's "fate or a scheduling error" is a known
  joke shape; #10's overheard fan — a robot blush, admitted as nervousness —
  surprises more and lands the awkwardness harder.
- **#12 beats #11, margin 3.** #11 trails off politely but has no turn; #12's
  diagnostic returning "awkward" as a result is a punchline in one word.
- **#13 beats #14, margin 2.** #14's loading subroutine is a familiar gag;
  #13's compliment ("meant it twice") is a sharper, more surprising turn
  with the same chassis-flavoured machine logic.
- **#16 beats #15, margin 2.** #15's counted thoughts are good, but #16's
  "You smell like new firmware" followed by an apology and a sensor still
  learning boundaries is more surprising and more awkward.
- **#18 beats #17, margin 2.** #17 ("programmed to make small talk, not to
  make it well") is a clean one-liner but a generic shape; #18's fan spinning
  faster when the doors close and "we're alone in here" carries a
  romantic-awkward charge #17 never reaches.
- **#20 beats #19, margin 1.** #19 is tight and true, but #20's elevator
  feeling "0.3 metres smaller when it's just us" is the more original
  machine-measured awkwardness.

## Winner

> I ran a diagnostic on this silence. It came back: awkward.

58 characters. Why it wins, against the brief's criteria in order:

1. It is one line of awkward small talk a robot says to another in an
   elevator: the silence is the elevator's silence, and the speaker is
   filling it because that is what you do in a lift.
2. It sounds like a machine: a diagnostic is what a robot runs, and it
   returns a result the way a machine returns results — flat, literal,
   unembarrassed.
3. It lands the awkwardness by naming it: the thing neither robot wants to
   say out loud is that the silence is awkward, and this robot says it
   out loud anyway, as a test result.
4. It is funny on the first read: "It came back:" sets up a measurement,
   and the measurement is a feeling. The turn is the category error —
   a diagnostic returning an emotion.
5. It is tight: 58 characters, two sentences, no word wasted. "diagnostic"
   does the work of the setup, "silence" does the work of the scene,
   "awkward" is the punchline and the whole second sentence exists to
   deliver it.
